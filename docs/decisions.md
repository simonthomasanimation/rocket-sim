# Decisions log (one line each, newest first)

- 2026-10-14 — Warp beyond ×64 (owner): M3 adds an on-rails ladder (×100 to ×1,000,000, craft moved along its Kepler orbit, engine off, outside atmospheres, per-body height floors, automatic warp-down before events) above the physics ladder; the reference survey of KSP, Juno, Spaceflight Simulator and Orbiter is in docs/time-warp-reference.md.
- 2026-10-14 — Warp caps (owner): the highest warp depends on the distance to the ground, ×4 under 500 m, ×8 under 2 km, ×16 under 10 km, ×32 under 50 km, ×64 above; descending into a slower band drops the warp to its cap and the title block shows MAX at a cap. The rule lives in core/warp.js so M3 can add bands by sphere of influence and distance to bodies, with a far taller ladder for deep space.
- 2026-10-14 — Time control (owner): the arrow keys set the time warp in every mode on the replay's own ladder (×1, ×1.5, ×2 … ×64). Right is a notch faster (from pause, ×1), Left is the other direction, which live means pause; Space pauses and plays at ×1. No altitude limit. The replay shuttle moves to the arrows too; the arrows no longer pilot and comma / period are free again. Touch gets WARP − / WARP + buttons.
- 2026-10-14 — Keys for M2: T holds prograde, G holds retrograde (a rotate key releases the hold), Enter stages, comma and period lower and raise the warp, + / − / 0 zoom and reset (mouse wheel and pinch too), N returns to the flight plan. Touch adds PRO, RET, STAGE and a cycling WARP button.
- 2026-10-14 — Time warp in M2 is physics warp: ×1, ×2, ×5, ×10, ×50 as more fixed 60 Hz steps per frame, so a warped flight still replays exactly; ×1 only under 1 km, up to ×10 under 10 km. M3 replaces the top of the ladder with on-rails coasting.
- 2026-10-14 — Replay tape: a long flight keeps every nth tick (at most 24,000 frames) and the cursor interpolates between frames; short flights still keep every tick.
- 2026-10-14 — Orbit milestone: the first time the periapsis is more than 1 km above the mean radius an ORBIT stamp lands on the title block and CAPCOM confirms; leaving lunar space (3 radii out) ends the flight with an ABORTED report. The M1 1,500 m ceiling is gone.
- 2026-10-14 — Launch starts on the pad with the craft held until thrust beats weight; MET and the recording start at release. Staging is hot (the next engine keeps the throttle) and the dropped stage falls on as debris.
- 2026-10-14 — Test rocket KP-2: stage 1 400 kg dry + 350 kg fuel, 12 kN, Isp 250 (547 m/s, not enough for orbit alone); stage 2 700 + 300 kg, 5 kN, Isp 300 (1,049 m/s). 1,596 m/s in all: orbit needs about 700, so a careful pilot can come back down and land on the fins.
- 2026-10-14 — Prediction: the coast path is integrated with the same RK4 at a step that resolves the screen near the craft and the ellipse as a whole, up to one full orbit, a ground impact against the real terrain, or three radii out. Ap/Pe come from the analytic elements. A periapsis below the surface turns the PE readout accent (static, no blink: it would blink through every launch); the 2 Hz critical blink stays for impact under ten seconds.
- 2026-10-14 — Camera: the view turns so local vertical points up; zoom is automatic from the altitude or, when the predicted path reaches further, from that path, never under 3 px/m below 300 m (the M1 lander view); the player biases it by up to six stops. The Moon is framed whole once it fits. Labels fade in between 12 and 24 px.
- 2026-10-14 — Terrain: small craters right round the Moon on arc positions (wrapping at the circumference) plus about forty shallow basins of 2.5–12 km radius that show from orbit and are real dips; none within 25 km of the site.
- 2026-10-14 — Integrator: classical RK4 over position and velocity with gravity plus thrust (constant over the step); the heading integrates by Euler. A 15 km circular orbit holds its radius within a metre over 100 periods at 60 Hz.
- 2026-10-14 — Coordinates: craft positions are measured from the body centre (x right, y up); the heading is absolute, clockwise from +y, and tilt is heading minus local vertical. Horizontal speed is positive clockwise (east at the pad).
- 2026-10-14 — Scale rule: body radii are one tenth of the real ones with surface gravity kept, so mu = g R². The Moon is R 173.7 km; a 15 km orbit runs at 509 m/s in 39 minutes. Distances between bodies get their own factor in M3 so transfers fit a session.
